# News clip video

1. **Lock the story.** Pick one question or consequential change. Prefer the event producer, filings, official transcript and original data. Record assertions and dated citations. Separate what the footage shows from the interpretation. Read [story-structures.md](references/story-structures.md) and choose a route from the viewer’s question and the available evidence. Write the voice script and dated storyboard before choosing shot lengths. Record the structure and evidence in the manifest; run `scripts/story_preflight.py` before going to render.
2. **Find and clear the shots.** Use web search to find original footage and official media archives; start with event, date, location and shot type. For NASA stories, the bundled discover.py retrieves public video candidates directly. For other stories, use official press pools, organization media pages, and Wikimedia Commons file pages; verify actual frames, source and access before selecting. A video being public to watch does not establish permission to edit or republish it. Read the exact asset's terms, including credit, commercial scope, third-party elements, and whether modifications are allowed. For a private preview, record footage reviewed for that use as a separate status. For outside distribution, only select assets separately cleared for the destination; an unknown right means use an original chart, timeline or source link instead. Use official downloads, never bypass access controls. Consult [production.md](references/production.md).
3. **Build an edit decision list.** Assign each beat a fact reference, exact source clip in/out, evidence role (direct footage, contextual archive, or illustration), caption, and credit. Open candidate shots and verify on representative frames. If using old footage, label its date and role on screen. Don't let a cut alter a quote’s meaning. Export credits and keep a separate manifest.
4. **Select narration.** For a stock voice, use the optional [offline Mandarin workflow](references/stock-voice.md) and visibly disclose AI narration. Use the user’s own voice only when authorized; for a one-off, prefer their actual recorded narration. To clone only the user's own voice, first obtain an explicitly authorized reference and any needed authorization for the selected service or cost. Prefer an available local model on the cloud computer. Never silently send a sample to a third party, reuse it across projects, or use a stand-in as the user's voice. While waiting, render a labeled preview without voice. Time captions to the final narration; confirm difficult names, numbers and punctuation by listening. Label AI-synthesized personal narration in the video or caption.
5. **Render and review.** Start with `scripts/broadcast.py` for a 1080p broadcast edit: opening and cut rhythm chosen by the story route, with wide footage, focused split layout, or graphics where they clarify the claim. Use original orchestral-electronic music with cut accents, never recognizable station jingles by default. It cuts exact source ranges, adds source credit and captions, and mixes user-authorized narration if provided. For a simpler layout, use `scripts/assemble.py`. `references/production.md` explains the inputs and QA. Watch the actual output, including cut points and the end, not just source thumbnails. Do not publish without separate authorization for the exact post.
6. **Paper transfer.** Share the evidence ledger, precise annotation placement and actual-output QA with the one-pager workflow. Only insert optional silent live footage inside a fixed window when the footage explains the source. Keep the A4 camera and layout fixed; montage, narration and fast cuts belong to this skill.
